Tutorial 06 · Processing the recordings

A first look at the night

Before averaging anything, look at the night. This page loads the tutorial night with the Portiloop analysis package, shows the whole night on one screen, adds an automatic sleep staging made offline with a small neural network (and shows why it cannot be trusted blindly), and walks through what the EEG, eye, chin and heart channels look like in wake, sleep, movement and at the moment the Cz electrode comes off at the end of the night.

Time
40 minutes
Level

In this tutorial you will

  • Load a recording with load_recording, choose the montage and read its summary; know what a Recording contains.
  • Read a whole-night spectrogram: wake alpha, the sigma band of spindles, cycles of slow-wave sleep, the stimulations, and the electrode that came off at the end.
  • Use an automatic sleep staging made offline with the NanoSleepNet model, and see on this night why automatic stages must be checked by an expert.
  • Recognize wake, N2, deep sleep, REM, a movement and an electrode coming off on all eight channels, including ECG, EOG and EMG.
  • Know which artifacts this night has and how Tutorial 07 deals with them.

Loading the night

Install the analysis package

The analysis code lives in examples/processing of the portiloop-software repository: a small Python package, portiloop_analysis, and two notebooks used in Tutorials 07 and 09. It needs only standard scientific Python:

git clone https://github.com/Portiloop/portiloop-software.git
cd portiloop-software/examples/processing
pip install -r requirements.txt      # numpy, scipy, pandas, matplotlib, jupyterlab, pytest

The notebooks import portiloop_analysis from this folder, "so start Jupyter here (or add this folder to PYTHONPATH)" (README of examples/processing). Put the unzipped tutorial dataset next to it, or give its full path below.

load_recording loads one channel and the stimulation markers. Its arguments:

  • files: one CSV, or a list of the CSVs of one night (after a Stop then Start). They are sorted by the date and time in their names and placed on a single timeline; "the time between two files is kept as missing data (NaN), so that event times stay correct" (docstring of load_recording).
  • channel and montage: the electrode you want, and which electrode is on which raw_ch column. Two montages are defined; "default" is the CLAS protocol montage. You can also pass the 8 names yourself.
  • fs: read from _metadata.json (250 Hz if the file is missing).
{'default': ('ECG1', 'ECG2', 'EOG1', 'EOG2', 'EMG', 'Cz', 'Fz', 'Fpz'),
 'no_ecg': ('Fpz', 'Cz', 'Pz', 'Fz', 'EOG1', 'EOG2', 'EMG1', 'EMG2')}
The montage is your responsibilityThe files only say raw_ch1 to raw_ch8 (Tutorial 05). load_recording(csv, channel="Cz", montage="no_ecg") would happily return raw_ch2, which in the CLAS montage is the ECG. The README of the package says it plainly: "Check them against the wiring of your cap."

The night at a glance

The best first look at a night is a spectrogram: time runs from left to right, frequency from bottom to top, and the colour gives the power of the EEG at that time and frequency (in decibels, so each step of 10 dB is a factor of 10 in power). Below it, the viewer stacks what the device did and the automatic sleep stages. It was computed from Cz, after a 0.3 to 35 Hz band-pass, as one Welch spectrum (4 s windows) per 30 s epoch, with the samples at the amplifier limit left out (docs/tools/build_assets.py).

The tutorial night: spectrogram, stages, stimulations

What to try: (1) "Falling asleep": find the band near 10 Hz and where it stops; (2) "Stimulation-rich W": the stages say awake while the spectrogram shows slow waves and the device keeps stimulating; (3) "The end": the dark block is Cz at the amplifier limit, and the green "detection running" strip stops a few seconds after it starts; (4) drag the plot sideways to move through the night, click it to read one 30 s epoch.

How to read it

  • Wake alpha (first 25 minutes). A band near 10 Hz from about minute 9 to minute 25: the alpha rhythm of relaxed wakefulness with the eyes closed. In those epochs 40 to 65% of the Cz power (0.5 to 30 Hz) is in the 8 to 12 Hz band; at minute 25.5 it drops to 10%, and the automatic staging moves to N1 at minute 28 and N2 at minute 31.5. The first 8 minutes are disturbed by large deflections on the eye channels (probably movements of the eyes and body; we cannot tell more from the file).
  • The sigma band. A faint horizontal band between about 12 and 15 Hz across most of the sleep period: sleep spindles, the 11 to 16 Hz bursts of N2 (and N3) sleep.
  • Slow-wave cycles. The bright bottom of the spectrogram (below 4 Hz) waxes and wanes: periods rich in slow waves alternate with periods without them, the cycles of NREM and REM sleep. The stimulations (pink bars) cluster in the slow-wave periods, as they should: the detector looks for large slow waves. Per hour of recording: 157, 301, 390, 210, 388, 361 and 123 stimulations.
  • Gaps. The longest stretch without any stimulation while detection was running goes from minute 202.4 to 220.3 (17.8 minutes), a period with little slow-wave power.
  • Vertical stripes. Short bright stripes across all frequencies are movements: an artifact has power everywhere.
  • The end. From minute 373.1, Cz is at the amplifier limit most of the time (red strip) and the spectrogram of Cz is dark: no Cz EEG is left (the other channels keep recording). Detection stops at minute 373.36 (green strip) for the rest of the recording.
Make the same plot from Python 
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from scipy.signal import spectrogram
from portiloop_analysis import filter_band

fs = recording.fs
cz = filter_band(recording.eeg, (0.3, 35), fs, notch_hz=60)  # zero-phase, mean removed
f, t, power = spectrogram(np.nan_to_num(cz), fs=fs, nperseg=int(4 * fs), noverlap=0)
keep = f <= 25
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 4))
plt.pcolormesh(t / 60, f[keep], 10 * np.log10(power[keep] + 1e-3), vmin=-10, vmax=30, shading="auto")
plt.plot(recording.events / fs / 60, np.full(len(recording.events), 24), "|", color="w")  # stimulations
plt.xlabel("Minutes from the start"); plt.ylabel("Frequency (Hz)"); plt.colorbar(label="dB")
plt.show()
It takes about a second after loading. Use notch_hz=50 where the mains is 50 Hz.

Automatic sleep staging with NanoSleepNet

The stages in the viewer were not scored by a person. They come from NanoSleepNet, a small neural network that stages each 30 s epoch (W, N1, N2, N3 or REM) from the Fpz minus Cz derivation, using that epoch and the 9 before it. It was trained on Sleep-EDF recordings (Fpz-Cz at 100 Hz) [2]. Its own README ends with a warning:

"Not validated on Portiloop recordings: no expert-scored Portiloop data was available."

README of the NanoSleepNet model
NanoSleepNet is not part of v2.3The model and its code come with the next software release (branch rdkx5), so docs/tools/stage_night.py needs that branch's code to run. Nothing else in these tutorials depends on it: the analyses of Tutorials 07 to 09 run with v2.3 and portiloop_analysis alone, and the stages are already in the dataset (_nanosleepnet.csv).

How the stages of the tutorial night were made

The script docs/tools/stage_night.py feeds the raw recording, 30 s at a time, to two classes of that release: Preprocessor (Fpz minus Cz, a 0.5 Hz high-pass, resampling from 250 to 100 Hz, clipping to ±192 µV, 30 s epochs; with the CLAS montage Fpz is raw_ch8 and Cz raw_ch6) and NanoSleepNetStep (the network, in NumPy).

python docs/tools/stage_night.py ../clas-tutorial_2026-11-15_04-01-29_CONDB/clas-tutorial_2026-11-15_04-01-29_CONDB.csv stages.csv
# 887 epochs staged in 9 s: stages.csv

The output has one row per 30 s epoch (epoch, start_s, stage, and p_W to p_REM, the probabilities of the five stages); it is identical to the _nanosleepnet.csv file of the dataset. The script stages every epoch of the file, including the last 70 minutes recorded with Cz at the amplifier limit.

To attach the stages to the recording, one stage per sample:

Why these stages must be checked

Three observations on this night show that the automatic staging is wrong in places:

  1. "Awake" epochs full of slow waves and sounds. More than a third of the stimulations (683) fall in epochs labelled W. Between minutes 235 and 280, 73 of the 90 epochs are labelled W (mean probability of W 0.76) and they contain 274 of the 313 stimulations of that period. During the stimulation period, the median Cz delta power (0.5 to 4 Hz) of the epochs labelled W is 441 µV², higher than that of the epochs labelled N2 (381 µV²) and 15 times that of the real wake of minutes 9 to 25 (30 µV²). Large slow waves and a stream of up-state detections are signs of NREM sleep, not of wakefulness.
  2. No N3 at all. The probability of N3 never exceeds 0.21 in the whole night, yet the excerpt "Deep sleep" below (minute 123, labelled N2) has slow waves down to −113 µV on Cz. Whether this night contains N3 can only be decided by a scorer.
  3. Stages without EEG. After minute 373 Cz is at the amplifier limit, so Fpz minus Cz is meaningless; the model still produces 64 REM, 37 W, 26 N2 and 14 N1 epochs there.

Why the model fails here we do not know. It was trained and evaluated on one dataset (Sleep-EDF), recorded with other amplifiers and electrodes, and never on Portiloop data; the sounds themselves, and their evoked responses, are also absent from its training data. These are possible reasons, not verified ones.

What to do in a studyUse the automatic stages to navigate a night, never as results. For analyses by sleep stage (Tutorials 07 and 09), have the nights scored by a trained scorer following the standard rules, using the EEG, EOG and chin EMG channels the protocol records. load_hypnogram reads such a scoring (a table with the columns Annotation and Duration). In Tutorial 07, results "by automatic stage" are shown only to illustrate the code.

What the signals look like

Six 30 s windows of the night, on all eight channels. Five are band-pass filtered from 0.3 to 35 Hz (zero phase, which removes the large electrode offsets and the mains); the last one, "Cz comes off", shows the raw signal with only its median over the previous minute removed. All of them are stored in steps of 0.1 µV, so values beyond ±3,276.7 µV are clipped.

Excerpt viewer: eight channels in six moments of the night

ECG on ch1 and ch2

The protocol places ECG2 (brown) on the right upper chest and ECG1 (black) on the left upper abdomen, but each is recorded against the same mastoid reference as the EEG. Each channel is therefore "chest electrode minus mastoid": ECG1 shows tall positive R waves (about 1.7 to 2 mV in the excerpts), ECG2 smaller negative ones. The usual bipolar lead is their difference:

data = pd.read_csv(csv, usecols=["raw_ch1", "raw_ch2"])
ecg = data["raw_ch1"] - data["raw_ch2"]   # bipolar ECG, black minus brown

In the sleep excerpts the heart beats at about 71 to 75 times per minute (R peaks counted in the N2, deep sleep and REM windows). The high impedances of these channels (177.5 and 156.0 kΩ at the check) do not matter much for a signal of millivolts, which is why the protocol accepts them.

EOG on ch3 and ch4

EOG1 sits above the outer corner of the left eye and EOG2 below the outer corner of the right eye. The eye is an electrical dipole (the cornea is positive with respect to the retina), so when both eyes move together, one electrode sees the cornea approach while the other sees it move away: eye movements give deflections of opposite sign on the two channels. Brain activity reaching both electrodes from the front of the head gives deflections of the same sign. The excerpts show it: below 2 Hz, the correlation between EOG1 and EOG2 is −0.61 in the wake window (eye movements) and +0.80 in the deep sleep window (frontal slow waves, which EOG1 follows closely: correlation +0.94 with Fpz). Fpz, close to the eyes, also picks up eye movements.

EMG on ch5

The chin electrode records muscle tone, again against the mastoid reference (a single electrode, not the bipolar chin EMG of clinical recordings). Muscle activity is mostly fast, so compare power at high frequencies: between 16 and 30 Hz, the EMG channel has 139 µV² in the wake window, 16 to 19 µV² in N2 and deep sleep, and 1.3 µV² in the REM window, the lowest of all, as expected from the loss of muscle tone in REM sleep. The 35 Hz low-pass of the viewer hides most of the EMG; for a real measure of muscle tone, filter the raw raw_ch5 above 30 Hz (the mains notch is then needed).

For neuroscientists: the device stimulates artifacts tooThe slow-oscillation detector checks that a wave is large enough (peak to peak above 75 µV, negative part beyond 40 µV) and has the right durations, but there is no upper limit on its amplitude (custom_detectors.py, lines 242 to 247). A movement on Cz can therefore pass the test, and the "Movement" excerpt shows 8 sounds sent at the start of, or during, a movement artifact. These events are real sounds heard by the participant, possibly during an arousal. They are rejected from the averages, but they are part of the stimulation the participant received: report how many there were, by condition.

Exercises

Load another channel

Load Fpz instead of Cz, and print its summary. Which column of the CSV does it come from, and what would happen with montage="no_ecg"?

Answer

load_recording(csv, channel="Fpz", montage="default") reads raw_ch8. With montage="no_ecg", Fpz is the first electrode, so it would read raw_ch1, which in this night is the ECG: the code cannot know, which is why the montage must match the wiring.

Find the sleep onset

In the night viewer, at which minute does the alpha band disappear, and how does that compare with the "Stimulation starts after" delay of this night?

Answer

Alpha dominates Cz until minute 25 and drops at minute 25.5; the automatic staging gives N1 from minute 28 and N2 from minute 31.5. The delay was 25 minutes, so detection started at minute 25.0, about when the participant fell asleep; the first stimulation came at minute 30.9. A longer delay (the protocol's example is 45 minutes) is safer for participants who take longer to fall asleep.

Would you trust "stimulations in N2"?

A colleague reports "1,157 stimulations in N2 and 683 in wake" for this night. What do you answer?

Answer

That these stages are automatic and not validated on Portiloop data, and that the "wake" epochs of the stimulation period have more slow-wave power than the "N2" epochs (median 441 vs 381 µV² at 0.5 to 4 Hz), against 30 µV² in real wake. The split by stage needs an expert scoring of the night.
